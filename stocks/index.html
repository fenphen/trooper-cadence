🎓 Learning tool, not advice. Nothing here buys, sells, or predicts. Technical indicators describe what a price has done; traders use them as a shared language, not a crystal ball. Signals are computed from bar closes on the selected time frame with textbook settings (see “How the signal works” below).

How the signal works

Five classic indicators each cast a vote: +1 bullish, −1 bearish, 0 neutral. The votes add up to a score from −5 to +5.

  • Score ≥ +2 → BUY (green). Most of the evidence points up.
  • Score ≤ −2 → SELL (red). Most of the evidence points down.
  • Otherwise → HOLD / WAIT (amber). The indicators disagree, so a trader waits for a clearer picture.

The five voters: trend (price vs. the 50‑ and 200‑bar averages), MACD, RSI, Bollinger Bands, and a recent golden/death cross. The bar under each price shows the votes.

Time frames. On 1D every bar is a trading day, so the 50‑ and 200‑bar averages are the famous 50‑ and 200‑day lines. On 1h/2h/4h each bar is that many hours of the regular session, so the same indicators describe days and weeks instead of months: day traders and swing traders watch these. The signal follows whichever time frame you pick.

Glossary

  • SMA 20 / 50 / 200 — simple moving averages: the average close over the last N trading days. Short averages track the price; long averages show the big trend.
  • Golden cross / death cross — the 50‑day average crossing above (golden) or below (death) the 200‑day average. Slow, but widely watched.
  • RSI (14) — Relative Strength Index, 0–100. Above 70 is “overbought” (rally may be stretched), below 30 “oversold” (sell‑off may be stretched).
  • MACD (12, 26, 9) — the gap between a fast and a slow average, plus a signal line. MACD above its signal = momentum rising.
  • Bollinger Bands (20, 2σ) — a channel two standard deviations around the 20‑day average. Price outside the bands is unusual.
  • 52‑week range — where today sits between the year's high and low.

Where the prices come from

The page is a plain web page with no server, so it asks free public feeds directly from your browser: Yahoo Finance's chart data (through a public relay, since Yahoo doesn't allow pages to call it directly), then Stooq for daily bars. If the feeds are down it shows the last data it saved on this device, and failing that a clearly labelled sample series so you can still explore the indicators.

Extended hours. The headline price is the latest trade including pre‑market (4:00–9:30 ET) and after‑hours (16:00–20:00 ET); the tag beside it says which session it came from, with the change measured from the regular close. Chart bars use the regular session only, as most charting tools do.

For a more dependable feed, add a free Twelve Data API key under Data settings; it stays in this browser only.

Changing tiles. Tap the ✎ on any tile to pick another stock from the popular list or search by name or ticker. The SpaceX tile looks for the ticker SPCX; if that symbol has no data on the feed, the tile says so instead of guessing.
